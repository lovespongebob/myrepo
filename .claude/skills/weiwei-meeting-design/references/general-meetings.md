# General Meetings: Decisions, Problems, Training, Hiring, Hard Conversations, Communities

Cross-domain meetings that anyone runs — in a company, a school, a nonprofit,
a neighborhood association, a family business. These templates are extensions
built with the book's method (the two-question job test, agenda math, the
four roles, divergence/convergence, silent voting to level power, pre-work
over room-work); the book's own catalog is project- and design-centric, so
this file covers the everyday meetings it leaves out. Use them whenever the
meeting isn't tied to a project stage or a leadership cadence.

## Decision meeting (30–60 min)

**Goal:** Make one named decision with the people who own it. Two failure
modes: arriving with a foregone conclusion (the meeting is theater), and
leaving with "more discussion needed" and no date (the meeting was a
conversation wearing a decision's clothes).
**Measure:** The decision is made and written down with its rationale and any
dissent noted — or explicitly deferred to a named date with what's missing.
"We'll keep talking" counts as failure.

**Prep:** a one-page framing sent ahead — the question, the options
considered, a recommendation, and what happens by default if nothing is
decided. Name the decision-maker and the decision rule up front (one owner
deciding with input, or a group vote). Invite only people who add information
or own the consequences.

**Agenda (45 min):**
- **Frame the decision (5)** — restate the question, options, decision rule,
  and the default if nothing is decided.
- **Test the options (15)** — surface what the framing missed: risks, missing
  options, criteria that matter. New options welcome here, nowhere later.
- **Weigh (15)** — score options against the surfaced criteria; silent
  positions first (dots or written stances), discussion after — commitments
  land before social pressure can bend them.
- **Decide & document (10)** — the owner or vote decides; write the decision,
  the why, the dissent, and the first action with an owner and date.

## Problem-solving session (45–90 min)

**Goal:** Move an urgent or stuck problem from confusion to owned next
actions. Facts first, causes second, fixes last — jumping to fixes before the
group agrees what's actually happening is the failure mode.
**Measure:** A shared statement of the problem, leading hypotheses each with
a test or fix, and every action owned and dated.

**Prep:** a visible board with three zones (timeline of facts / hypotheses /
actions); a facilitator and a recorder even in a hurry — *especially* in a
hurry; whoever holds relevant facts is in the room, and spectators aren't.

**Agenda (60 min):**
- **Facts & timeline (15)** — what is observably true, in time order, on the
  board. Corrections welcome; interpretations wait.
- **Hypotheses (15)** — every plausible cause, one per sticky; group
  duplicates; nothing gets shot down yet (divergence).
- **Converge on causes (15)** — rank hypotheses by evidence and cost-to-test;
  pick the few worth acting on.
- **Actions (15)** — per chosen hypothesis: the test or fix, the owner, the
  date — and when this group reconvenes.

## Training session (1–3 hours)

**Goal:** People leave able to do something they couldn't before. Straight
lecture is the failure mode — the memory research in
`agenda-building.md` applies directly: after 20–30 minutes of pure listening,
absorption falls off a cliff.
**Measure:** Learners demonstrate the skill before leaving — an exercise
completed, a checklist run, a cold question answered. Attendance measures
nothing.

**Prep:** cut content to what fits (5 concepts per 10 minutes, then halve it
to leave room for practice); an exercise per chunk, never one big one at the
end; complementary visuals, never slides read aloud.

**Agenda (90 min):**
- **Why this matters (10)** — the job the skill does for the learner, and
  what they'll be able to do by the end.
- **Teach chunk 1 (20)** — ≤5 concepts, visuals paired with talk.
- **Practice chunk 1 (15)** — learners apply it immediately; the instructor
  circulates; mistakes here are the point.
- **Teach chunk 2 (20)** — ≤5 more concepts, building on the first.
- **Practice chunk 2 (15)** — a second application with less scaffolding.
- **Demonstrate & close (10)** — each learner shows the skill or answers a
  cold question; name where help lives afterward. Break before the 90-minute
  mark in longer sessions.

## Hiring interview & debrief (45–90 min)

**Goal:** Judge a candidate against agreed criteria — and protect the
judgment from anchoring and from the loudest voice in the debrief.
**Measure:** Every criterion scored with evidence by every interviewer
*independently, before any discussion*; the debrief ends hire / no-hire / a
specific follow-up, never "mixed feelings."

**Prep:** criteria and questions divided among interviewers ahead — nobody
freelances the same three questions; independent written scores submitted
before the debrief (divergent scores are where the debrief spends its time —
the same high-standard-deviation trick as kickoff expectation scoring);
reserve time for the candidate's questions, because they're assessing fit
too.

**Agenda (interview 45 + debrief 20):**
- **Their story (10)** — the candidate's path in their words; listen for the
  criteria rather than chasing tangents.
- **Structured questions (25)** — each interviewer's assigned criteria, with
  follow-ups for evidence: "walk me through a time…".
- **Their questions (10)** — answer honestly; mis-selling costs more later.
- **Debrief: silent scores (5)** — scores and evidence submitted before
  anyone speaks.
- **Debrief: work the gaps (15)** — discuss only criteria where scores
  diverge; end with the decision or a named follow-up.

## Difficult conversation — feedback 1:1 (30–45 min)

**Goal:** Say a hard thing so it can be heard, and understand before
persuading. Built on the nonviolent communication approach from
`facilitation.md`: observations, feelings, needs, requests — never verdicts
about the person.
**Measure:** Both people can state the other's view accurately, and each
leaves with one concrete change or request. Agreement is optional;
understanding isn't.

**Prep:** write the opener as observation + impact + request, stripped of
judgments ("when X happened," never "when you were careless"); pick a private
time with nothing stacked right after; decide what you actually need from the
conversation — venting isn't a need.

**Agenda (30 min):**
- **The observation (5)** — the specific, observable thing and its impact;
  no character claims, no "always" or "never."
- **Their side (10)** — listen to understand, checking as you go: "It sounds
  like… is that right?" Their account may change yours.
- **Needs & requests (10)** — what each of you needs; clear positive requests
  ("could you flag risks on Mondays?") instead of demands.
- **Agree the change (5)** — one concrete change each, and when you'll check
  how it's going.

## Community / committee meeting (60–90 min)

**Goal:** A group of equals — volunteers, neighbors, a board, a club — makes
progress without the meeting being democratic to a fault: everyone heard,
strong personalities contained, quiet people counted. (This is the situation
that started the book: community meetings where discussion was endless and
the loudest voices won.)
**Measure:** Motions resolved — passed, rejected, or tabled to a date — and
actions owned by name. The same items reappearing meeting after meeting means
the meeting isn't doing its job.

**Prep:** a proposal-driven agenda published ahead — items with a named
sponsor and a clear ask beat "open discussion"; timeboxes per item, visible
to all; a rotating facilitator and a public recorder; written or silent
voting available for anything contested, because it levels the room.

**Agenda (60 min):**
- **Open & minutes (5)** — confirm last meeting's actions in one pass: done,
  not done, carried.
- **Proposals (35)** — each sponsored item: the ask (2 min), clarifying
  questions, timeboxed discussion, then vote or table with a date.
- **Open floor (15)** — new topics, timeboxed per speaker; anything
  substantial becomes a sponsored proposal for next time.
- **Actions & close (5)** — read back every action with its owner; confirm
  next meeting's proposal deadline.
