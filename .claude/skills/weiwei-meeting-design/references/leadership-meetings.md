# Leadership Meetings: 1:1s, Staff Meetings, Reviews, All-Hands, Readouts

The recurring meetings a manager or executive runs — from a first-line manager
to an SVP with an org of thousands. These templates extend the book's method
into executive contexts; they are applications of its principles (the
two-question job test, agenda math, the four roles, divergence/convergence,
pre-work over room-work) rather than templates the book itself contains. Use
them whenever the user's meeting is an ongoing organizational one rather than
a project-stage one.

Two ideas do the heavy lifting at this level:

- **Status moves to writing.** In every recurring leadership meeting, the
  failure mode is spending synchronous time on information transfer that a
  pre-read, a dashboard, or a doc handles better. The room is for questions,
  decisions, coaching, and conflict — the things that need synchrony.
- **Scale changes the physics.** Up to ~7 people you have a conversation;
  to ~40 you have engineered subgroups; beyond that you have a broadcast,
  where the points-of-agreement model no longer applies and alignment comes
  from chunked content (≤5 concepts, pause, next), complementary visuals,
  and structured, upvoted Q&A in place of open discussion.

The kill test still applies to every one of these: why was this recurring
meeting established, and is it still doing that job? An inherited staff
meeting or review that nobody can justify gets redesigned or deleted like any
other standing meeting.

## 1:1 — manager ↔ individual (25–45 min)

**Goal:** The individual's meeting, not the manager's: unblock decisions,
coach, and hear what dashboards can't say. Status lives in writing — reading
status aloud wastes the 1:1's one advantage, which is candor.
**Measure:** The individual sets at least half the agenda; blockers leave
unblocked or escalated; a growth topic gets real time at least monthly. A 1:1
that is all status, or all manager-talking, is failing even if it feels
pleasant.

**Prep:** a shared running doc both sides add topics to during the week;
status moved to writing before the meeting.

**Agenda (30 min):**
- **Their agenda (12)** — the individual's topics first, in their order.
- **Blockers needing your authority (8)** — decisions, escalations, and
  cross-org friction only a manager can move.
- **Coaching & growth (8)** — career direction, feedback in both directions,
  one situation to work through together.
- **Actions (2)** — each side says what they're taking away; log it in the
  shared doc.

**Skip-level variant:** same structure, two changes. State up front that
nothing travels upward with attribution without permission — the meeting's
value is unfiltered signal, and signal dies without that guarantee. Measure it
by new insights and risks surfaced that the management chain hadn't already
told you.

## Leadership staff meeting (45–90 min)

**Goal:** Your direct-report team decides together and manages cross-team
dependencies. Round-robin status is the failure mode: status is a pre-read,
and the room is for decisions.
**Measure:** Decisions made per meeting, dependencies resolved, and a written
cascade — what each leader tells their own org afterward. If the same
questions come back week after week, the cascade isn't landing.

**Prep:** status and metrics as a pre-read at least a day ahead; decision
items nominated in advance, each with a one-page framing (the situation, the
options, a recommendation); rotate the facilitator among the team monthly so
no one person owns "the truth."

**Agenda (60 min):**
- **Pre-read Q&A (10)** — questions on the status pre-read only; anything
  needing real discussion becomes a future decision item.
- **Decision items (25)** — two or three maximum, pre-framed; decide, or
  defer explicitly with a date. An hour cannot hold more genuine decisions
  than that (agenda math: each is several just-complex-enough concepts plus
  discussion).
- **Cross-team dependencies (15)** — each leader names what they need from
  another team; pair owners for anything deeper than a sentence.
- **Cascade & actions (10)** — agree the message each leader carries to
  their org, verbatim where wording matters; log actions with owners.

## Key business review (QBR/MBR) (1–2 hours)

**Goal:** Judge business health against targets and reallocate attention,
people, and money. The slide parade is the failure mode: the deck is a
pre-read; the room works the exceptions.
**Measure:** Reallocation decisions made and risks assigned owners; every red
metric leaves with a recovery plan or an explicit, on-the-record acceptance.
Silent acceptance of a red metric is the review failing at its one job.

**Prep:** the full review pack distributed at least two working days ahead —
metrics against targets with variance explained; presenters prepare the *why*
behind exceptions, never a re-reading of the numbers; cap the speaking bench
at ~7 (the conversation cap), with others attending as observers.

**Agenda (90 min):**
- **Silent review & questions (10)** — re-skim the pack in the room; collect
  questions visibly, no answers yet.
- **Exceptions: red & yellow (30)** — work only what's off-target: root
  cause, trajectory, what help is needed. Greens get thirty seconds each.
- **Deep dive (25)** — one or two risks the room picks (dot vote if
  contested), explored properly with divergence before convergence.
- **Decisions & reallocation (15)** — resource asks decided or dated; risks
  get owners; acceptance is explicit.
- **Actions (10)** — owners, dates, and what changes in the next review's
  pack. Insert a break if the booking exceeds 90 minutes.

## All-hands / org broadcast (45–60 min)

**Goal:** Move the same understanding into hundreds or thousands of heads and
take the org's questions seriously. One-to-many: trust is built by what
leadership answers, not by what it presents.
**Measure:** Question volume and coverage (asked vs. answered), plus a pulse
check on "I understand where we're going." Applause measures nothing.

**Broadcast physics:** the points-of-agreement model doesn't apply — don't
try to "discuss" with 2,000 people. Presentation capped at 20–30 minutes of
actual listening (attention decays hard after that), chunked into groups of
≤5 concepts with a pause between chunks; complementary visuals, never slides
read aloud. Pre-submitted questions with upvoting, opened days ahead — the
voted list *is* the Q&A agenda, playing the role dot voting plays in a room.
Record it, and publish a written summary the same day.

**Agenda (50 min):**
- **Open: the one message (5)** — the single thing everyone should be able
  to repeat afterward, stated plainly up front.
- **The state of things (20)** — the chunked presentation.
- **Recognition (5)** — specific work, named people, why it mattered.
- **Voted Q&A (15)** — top-voted questions first, the hard ones included;
  skipping an uncomfortable question costs more trust than any answer could.
- **Close: Monday behavior (5)** — what anyone should do differently
  starting Monday; where the summary and recording will land.

## Executive readout — presenting up (30–45 min)

**Goal:** Get a decision or endorsement from senior leadership (the ELT, a
board, a budget council). The failure mode is the real-estate tour — walking
the work screen by screen while the actual ask stays unstated until minute
25, which is exactly how the book's opening story (Jim and the VPs) goes
wrong.
**Measure:** The decision obtained, or a named blocker with a dated next
step. Anything else was a status email wearing a meeting.

**Prep:** pre-read at least two working days ahead, and the meeting assumes
it was read. **Pre-wire**: short 1:1s with the key voices beforehand — the
"meetings before the meeting" — so objections surface where they can be
worked; a surprise in the room means the preparation failed. Know your
walk-away: the minimum decision that keeps things moving.

**Agenda (35 min):**
- **The ask, first (5)** — state the decision needed and your recommendation
  in the first two minutes; context only as needed.
- **The case (10)** — at most 5 points: evidence, options considered, risks
  owned honestly.
- **Structured discussion (15)** — collect questions before answering any;
  answer simple → complex; capture objections visibly so they're addressed
  rather than re-raised.
- **Confirm & close (5)** — restate the decision as understood, aloud; name
  the next step, owner, and date before leaving the room.

Golden tickets don't work upward — you can't hand your CEO a sticky-note
allowance. The equivalent controls are the pre-wire, the ask-first structure,
and visible capture of objections.
