# Deep Report Mode

The light brief answers "what should I think about this industry" in three minutes. The deep report answers "show me the work" — same six-step framework, each step expanded into a full section with its evidence on the table. Use it when the user chose Deep in the intake, asked for a full report / deep dive / detailed analysis, or when the decision at stake plainly justifies the length (committing capital, entering a market).

Everything from the light mode still binds: confidence tags on every claim, the evidence rules from the cold-start playbook, goal-angling, and honest gaps ("unknown / to verify") instead of filled-in guesses. Depth means more evidence shown, never more words per fact.

## Report structure

Trim sections that don't serve the user's goal (a career-move report doesn't need an opportunity shortlist); keep the numbering of what remains. Open every deep report with section 1 — a reader who stops there should still get the answer.

1. **Decision summary** — the question, the verdict with its conditions, the 2–3 pieces of evidence it leans on hardest, the single biggest unknown, and the next action. Half a page at most.
2. **Scope and boundary** — classification standard chosen and why; the value chain as a diagram (Mermaid) or table: roles, who delivers what to whom, who pays, who controls the key entry points; the exact segment analyzed and what's excluded.
3. **Life cycle and stage** — the penetration series (or proxy arithmetic), the stage call, and both cross-checks (revenue growth, concentration) shown; what would move the call to the neighboring stage.
4. **How the industry runs** — representative business models: what customers buy, who pays and why, revenue and cost structure, pricing power; unit economics on the smallest repeatable unit; where margin concentrates along the chain.
5. **Market size** — the sizing ladder with a recomputable table: every variable gets an ID, definition, value or range, unit and period, source or assumption tag. TAM by the four-factor formula; SAM under the stated serviceability constraints; SOM from reachable customers × supportable win rate, capped by delivery capacity. State the metric (units, revenue, transactions) and never compare figures on different metrics. Scenarios only when a variable genuinely swings the answer — name the sensitive variable rather than manufacturing three-point precision.
6. **Competitive landscape and barriers** — CR3/CR8 with sources; moat type per leader; for entry goals, a barrier table: each barrier, its impact, the user's known gap, and how to verify or close it — no invented advantages.
7. **External drivers and risks** — the PEST screen as a table separating happened / announced / forecast, each with its transmission path to a specific role or link; converged to the 3 core drivers/risks with the reasoning visible.
8. **Segments and pain points** *(entering or selling-to goals)* — segment groups with the user, the payer, and the purchase decision-maker identified per segment; current alternatives and switching costs; structural pain vs. individual complaints.
9. **Opportunity shortlist** *(entering or selling-to goals)* — each candidate as: target segment → problem → who pays → entry point → supporting evidence and counter-evidence → key assumption → verdict (verify first / hold / insufficient evidence) → smallest verification action with its pass/kill signal. Reasons for holds shown next to reasons for picks.
10. **Tracking plan** — the 1–2 indicators, their free sources, publication cadence, current direction, and the 3-month divergence rule written as an explicit trigger.
11. **Evidence register** — a closing table: evidence ID, the claim it supports, source and publisher, publication date, data-coverage period, limitations, and conflicts (kept side by side with the adopted basis stated). Every [Sourced] tag in the body should trace to a row here.

## Completion standards

- Section 1 alone answers the question; sections 2–11 let a skeptic re-derive it.
- Every number in section 5 recomputes from its variable table — a reader with a calculator gets the same result.
- Unknowns appear as "unknown / to verify" with their impact stated, never silently filled.
- A deep report in report mode cites the provided reports by name per claim; in cold-start mode it leans on the evidence register and marks the vintage of recalled figures.
- Length serves evidence: a typical deep report runs 4–8 pages of markdown. Past that, cut restatement, never cut evidence.
